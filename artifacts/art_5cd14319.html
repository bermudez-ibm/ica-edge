Why it's hard today
Planning runs on estimated inputs
Product line managers build assortments to a GM target, but pricing and demand are only estimated — with limited collaboration with the geo teams that own pricing and marketplace relationships.
Futures lock decisions in early
Most product is bought on futures — locking demand to a geo, partner, and price point far ahead — and ships at season start with little holdback flex. Cost spans factory, materials, freight & duty, each moving independently.
Forecasting alone doesn't defend margin
Nike must see GM impact by country of origin, demand, and trade-rate — and, more importantly, operationalize actions across many functions, not forecast in isolation.

02

The capability, in six parts

Tariff Resilience is one integrated capability with six connected components. The first three sense; the second three decide and act. Together they close the loop from signal to margin.

🌐

1 · Tariff & landed-cost sensing

A living model of true landed cost by SKU and country of origin, that re-prices instantly as tariff rates and trade rules move — so the cost picture is never stale.

📈

2 · Causal demand sensing

Consumer- and SKU-level demand signals powered by causal AI (IBM Research), so forecasts explain why demand moves — not just extrapolate the past.

🧱

3 · Enterprise data foundation

The connected data layer that unifies demand, supply, cost, and consumer signals — rebuilt to the resolution the new operating model needs.

🧭

4 · Intelligent sourcing & allocation

Scenario-based decisions on where to make and where to ship product, balancing landed cost, lead time, and demand — with tariff exposure as a live input.

🏷️

5 · Dynamic pricing & margin defense

Precision pricing and sell-through decisions that defend margin and hold the line on premium, not promotional — across digital and physical.

⚙️

6 · Decisioning & governance

The operating cadence and governance — with four geo proof points — that turns signals into decisions people actually make, on time.

03

A draft program delivery approach

Deliberately staged: a bounded, low-risk first move that proves the model, then a phased build to a capability fully embedded by the time FY28 becomes Nike's first "normal year."

Phase 1
10-week sprint
Fixed-fee · half at risk

Prove the model — a bounded diagnostic sprint

  • Tariff & landed-cost sensing diagnostic across representative SKUs and origins
  • Causal-model design for demand and pricing on a focused product/geo slice
  • Program economics & governance blueprint — the multi-year business case
  • Team: 5 named IBM practitioners + 3 Nike delegates; senior IBM Research voice on causal AI
Phase 2
~2 quarters
Foundational build

Build the core — sensing engine + data foundation

  • Stand up the landed-cost and causal demand-sensing engines on live data
  • Establish the enterprise data foundation connecting demand, supply, cost, and consumer signals
  • Wire the first decisioning loop into a single geo as the reference implementation
Phase 3
~3–4 quarters
Scale across geos

Scale — four geo proof points

  • Extend to all four geos, tuned to each geo's operating model (incl. Greater China local-for-local)
  • Activate sourcing/allocation and dynamic-pricing decisioning across the network
  • Align to geo-level accountability so each GM sees the capability in their own P&L
Phase 4
FY28-ready
Embedded capability

Embed — a standing operating capability

  • Governance and cadence run natively inside Nike's teams — IBM steps back to steward
  • Sensing-and-decisioning operating at the resolution Sport Offense requires from FY28
  • Continuous improvement loop: signal → decision → margin, measured every cycle

Why the first move looks the way it does

A 10-week, fixed-fee sprint with half the fee at risk against agreed, deck-ready outputs. Small enough to approve inside the existing run-rate, structured so the commitment runs both ways — and timed so its output is ready for the Investor Day narrative window.

The business benefits, fully deployed

What Nike gets from the fully embedded capability — the financial value that shows up in the P&L, and the strategic value that compounds beyond any single quarter.

💵 Financial benefits
  • Margin protection under the tariff regime
    Intelligent sourcing, allocation, and pricing keep the 10%→15% incremental tariff from being absorbed straight into gross margin.
  • Reduced markdown & promotional leakage
    Precision sell-through decisions defend full-price realization — direct support for "premium, not promotional."
  • Working-capital & inventory efficiency
    Sharper demand sensing reduces both excess inventory and stockouts, freeing cash and improving turns.
  • Landed-cost optimization
    Sourcing and allocation decisions made against live landed cost — not last season's assumptions — lower the true cost to serve.
  • A quantified, peer-scale mitigation program
    A named, dollar-sized program in the order of magnitude peers have already committed to publicly — Nike's own version, sized to Nike's scale.
🎯 Non-financial benefits
  • Capability rebuilt to the right resolution
    Sensing-and-decisioning restored and modernized to match the SKU-, country-, and consumer-level grain the operating model now runs at.
  • A credible Investor Day proof point
    A named, concrete operating answer to the tariff-and-margin question — the kind of substance a once-a-decade framing moment rewards.
  • Geo-leader enablement
    Four geo proof points give each GM the sensing and decisioning tools to own their revenue and profit under the new incentive structure.
  • Reduced capability & key-person risk
    A standing, governed capability — not tribal knowledge — so decision quality no longer depends on who's in the room.
  • Speed of decisioning
    Forecast, make, deliver — on time. The loop from signal to decision to action gets measurably faster, cycle over cycle.
